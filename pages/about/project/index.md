# What is Globalise?

<!-- Sources: https://globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/project_overview/ ; https://globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/source-corpus/ ; https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/mission/mission_researchthemes/ (consulted 2026-10-01) -->

Globalise is a research project that builds a digital research infrastructure for the archives of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). It focuses on one archival series in particular: the *Overgekomen Brieven en Papieren* (OBP), or "letters and papers received". This research portal is where the results of that work come together.

## The archive

Between 1602 and 1799, the VOC built a network of trading posts, fortifications and colonies stretching from the Cape of Good Hope and the Red Sea to Japan. Officials throughout this network reported to the company's Asian headquarters in Batavia (present-day Jakarta), which sent copies of these letters, reports, journals and other documents to the Dutch Republic each year. The OBP is the collection of these documents as they arrived in the Netherlands.

The series is now held by the Nationaal Archief (Dutch National Archives) in The Hague and runs to almost 5 million handwritten pages. It covers a region that includes present-day Yemen, Iran, India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Indonesia, Taiwan, Japan and parts of China, as well as Madagascar, the east coast of Africa and the area around Cape Town. For some of these regions, the OBP contains some of the earliest surviving written sources.

The documents deal with far more than trade. They report on war and diplomacy, on the rulers and states the VOC dealt with, on taxation, plantations and forced labour, on slavery and the slave trade, on religion, disease, weather and famine. Read critically, they are a source not only for the history of the VOC but also for the histories of the societies it encountered and often subjugated.

For a fuller introduction to the VOC, its archives and their structure, see the [guide to the VOC archives](/pages/research-responsibly/voc-archives-guide.md).

## The problem

The importance of the OBP has long been recognised, and earlier projects have made parts of it more accessible. Between 1960 and 2017, the Huygens Institute and its predecessors published annotated selections of the *Generale Missiven* (General Letters), the summarising reports sent from Batavia to the company's directors. These fill 14 volumes but cover only about one-sixth of the material. The TANAP project ("Towards a New Age of Partnership") produced descriptions of documents in VOC archives around the world.

Even so, most of the OBP could only be read page by page, in the original handwriting, in seventeenth- and eighteenth-century Dutch. Finding every mention of a particular ruler, place, commodity or group of people across millions of pages was practically impossible. This has shaped which histories have been written from these archives, and which have not.

## What Globalise does

The Nationaal Archief has digitised almost all of its VOC archives. Building on these scans, the Globalise project has:

* **transcribed** the almost 5 million pages of the OBP using handwritten text recognition (HTR) software;
* **identified** people, places, polities (states and other political entities), commodities and other entities in the text, as well as events such as conflicts, treaties and voyages, using a combination of manual annotation and language models;
* **created reference data and thesauri**: curated datasets and vocabularies on polities, rulers, places, people, commodities, weights and measures, and other concepts that appear in the archive, with special attention to non-European actors;
* **linked** these elements together, so that a mention in a document can be connected to what is known about that entity elsewhere.

The [Methods](/pages/about/methods.md) page explains these steps in more detail.

The aim is to move beyond *searchability*, being able to find a word in the text, towards *researchability*: being able to ask historical questions of the archive and receive results with context. Globalise was set up to support new, more diverse and more representative histories based on the VOC archives, especially histories of the societies of Asia and Africa that have often been treated as background to the story of the company. The [Project & ambitions](/pages/about/project/ambitions.md) page explains this in more detail.

## Who is behind Globalise?

Globalise is based at the Huygens Institute in Amsterdam and is funded by the Dutch Research Council (NWO). It is carried out together with the International Institute of Social History (IISH), the KNAW Humanities Cluster, the University of Amsterdam, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam and the Nationaal Archief. See [Team & partners](/pages/about/project/team.md) for the people and institutions involved.
