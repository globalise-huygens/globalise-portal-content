# Ethics & Colonial Context

<!-- Sources: pages/research-responsibly/voc-archives-guide.md; Globalise Ethics Policy v2.0 (https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/ethics/policy/); "Mission, Research Themes and Guiding Questions" v1.0 (pages/about/project/archive/mission-research-themes.md). -->

The documents on this portal come from the archives of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), a trading company that was also a colonial power. They were written by and for the company, by people with their own political, religious and racial prejudices and agendas. They are an invaluable source for the history of Asia, Africa and the Indian Ocean world, but they cannot be read at face value.

Much of what you find on the portal has also been produced by computational methods: automatic transcriptions, entities and events recognised by language models, and links to reference data. These methods make mistakes, and they can repeat or strengthen the biases of the sources.

Critical interpretation is therefore essential. The pages in this section explain why, and what that means in practice.

## A note on sensitive content

The VOC archives record a period of history marked by violence. They contain records of enslavement, war, judicial punishment and exploitation, and they use language and descriptions of people that are offensive and painful to read. You may encounter such material anywhere on this portal, including in search results.
