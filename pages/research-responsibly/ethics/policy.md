# Ethics policy

<!-- Source: Mrinalini Luthra and Amber Zijlma, "GLOBALISE Ethics Policy", version 2.0, 19 February 2025, archived at /pages/about/archive/ethics-policy.md (originally https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/ethics/policy/). Licence: CC BY 4.0. The summary below is an editorial condensation. -->

The Globalise project worked with colonial archives and built a digital infrastructure that shapes how those archives are searched and read. The project's ethics policy set out the principles that guided this work, and the obligations team members took on towards each other, towards the people and communities whose histories the archive documents, and towards the users of the infrastructure.

This page summarises the policy and shows where on this portal you can see how its principles were put into practice. The full text of the policy is preserved in the archive: [GLOBALISE Ethics Policy](/pages/about/archive/ethics-policy.md). It was first published in 2025 on the project's former documentation website.

## Summary

The policy was written by Mrinalini Luthra and Amber Zijlma. Version 2.0 dates from February 2025. It was meant as a living document, to be revisited and revised, and was shared so that other projects could adapt it. It should be read together with the [ethical commitments of the Combatting Bias project](https://combattingbias.huygens.knaw.nl/about/ethicalcommitments/), which address institutional and systemic injustices.

The policy is built around six principles:

* **Diversity, equity and inclusion** in the project's people, governance, perspectives, datasets, algorithms and interfaces. This includes attention to bias and to groups that have been historically disadvantaged, participation of stakeholders and community experts (with fair compensation), and a commitment to a safe digital space: the sources contain offensive language and harmful descriptions, which the project addresses through historical contextualisation, documentation, interface design and content warnings.
* **Transparency** about data sources, algorithms, decisions and governance, through documentation (such as data-envelopes for datasets and model cards for language models), clear communication of the system's limitations, and open, FAIR publication of data, code and research.
* **Accountability** for the project's decisions and their consequences, including provenance information for the data Globalise produces, mechanisms for users to raise concerns, and reflexivity about the project's position as a well-funded research project in the Global North working with an archive that embodies Eurocentric perspectives.
* **Societal and environmental wellbeing**: warning users about offensive content and contextualising violent events with sensitivity towards affected groups, and limiting the project's environmental footprint, for example by offering lighter versions of models and datasets.
* **Robustness** of the AI models used, through careful curation of training and test data and analysis of model output for discriminatory or biased results.
* **Privacy and data governance**: no collection of personal user information, and storage of research data in certified trusted repositories.

The policy also introduces an **ethics report**: a template for assessing each part of the project against these six principles. Two worked examples are included, one for the source corpus and one for the places dataset.

## From policy to practice

The policy was written while the infrastructure was being built. Several of its principles are reflected on other pages of this portal:

* **Diversity, equity and inclusion; societal wellbeing.** How the project dealt with offensive terms, colonial categories and place names is described under [Colonial context](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/colonial-context.md). The note on sensitive content is in [Ethics & colonial context](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/index.md#a-note-on-sensitive-content).
* **Transparency.** Data sources, models and methods are documented under [How the data was produced](/pages/research-responsibly/data/production.md) and [Technical documentation](/pages/research-responsibly/data/technical-documentation.md). The limitations of the data are described under [Bias & limitations](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/bias-and-limitations.md).
* **Accountability.** Every annotation records how it was made: see [Provenance](/pages/research-responsibly/data/data-model.md#provenance).
* **Robustness.** Possible bias in the models is described under [Bias in models](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/bias-and-limitations.md#bias-in-models).
* **Privacy and data governance.** See the [privacy statement](/pages/footer/privacy.md) and [Long-term availability](/pages/research-responsibly/data/access.md#long-term-availability).
