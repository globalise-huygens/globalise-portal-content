# Responsible use

<!-- Sources: pages/research-responsibly/voc-archives-guide.md (section "What do you need to be aware of when using historical archives like the records of the VOC?"); "Mission, Research Themes and Guiding Questions" v1.0, section "Harnessing Big Data Responsibly" (pages/about/project/archive/mission-research-themes.md); Globalise Ethics Policy v2.0 (pages/research-responsibly/ethics/policy.md). General advice on reading the VOC archives critically is in the VOC archives guide; this page links to it and adds what is specific to the portal. -->

Globalise makes it possible to search millions of pages of colonial archives in ways that were not possible before. That brings new opportunities, and new responsibilities. The project has tried to be transparent about its sources, methods and limitations, but it cannot anticipate every way in which the material will be used. This page offers practical guidance for reading, interpreting and reusing what you find.

## Read critically

The [guide to the VOC archives](/pages/research-responsibly/voc-archives-guide.md#what-do-you-need-to-be-aware-of-when-using-historical-archives-like-the-records-of-the-voc) explains what to keep in mind when reading the VOC archives, and lists the questions to ask of any document: what its purpose was, who wrote it and for whom, where else the same events were recorded, and which other sources exist, inside and outside the VOC archives.

On the portal, these questions start with a search result. A result is a small piece taken from a much larger document, and it only makes sense in the context of that document. Before you draw conclusions from it, open the document it comes from and read the surrounding pages. Keep in mind, too, that the portal makes the VOC archives easier to use than many other sources on the same subjects; it does not make them more complete.

## Distinguish source from data

Keep track of what you are looking at:

* the original document, held by the Nationaal Archief (Dutch National Archives);
* the scan of that document;
* the automatic transcription of the scan;
* entities and events recognised by language models;
* reference data and thesauri curated by the project team.

Each of these involves a different degree of interpretation and a different kind of possible error. In particular, do not present a model-generated annotation as an established historical fact. Check transcriptions against the scan, and annotations against the text. See [Bias & limitations](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/bias-and-limitations.md).

## Be mindful of harmful content

The archives contain descriptions of violence, enslavement and exploitation, and language that is offensive and painful to read. This can affect you as a reader, and it can affect others when you quote, publish or present the material. Consider the people and communities whose histories these documents concern, and provide context when you reproduce offensive terms or descriptions. See [Colonial context](/pages/research-responsibly/ethics/colonial-context.md#colonial-terminology-and-categories).
