# Related projects and databases

The Globalise project built on the work of many earlier projects, and Globalise covers only part of the VOC archives. This page points to projects, databases and resources that complement the portal.

## TANAP: Towards a New Age of Partnership

<!-- Source: https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/ and its source repository https://github.com/globalise-huygens/tanap.docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl (consulted 2026-10-01). -->

TANAP (Towards a New Age of Partnership) was an international programme, launched in the late 1990s, to preserve the archives of the Dutch East India Company (VOC) and make them more accessible. It was led by the Nationaal Archief (Dutch National Archives) and Leiden University, and worked with archival institutions in Asia and South Africa. Besides creating finding aids and transcriptions, TANAP provided scholarships, supported archival training, and organised workshops and conferences. It also contributed to the successful nomination of the VOC archives to UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.

The TANAP website is no longer online. The Globalise project made a selection of its materials available again on the [TANAP resources website](https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/).

### Archival inventories of VOC-related collections worldwide

The VOC archives are not kept in one place. Each VOC settlement kept its own records, and those that survive are now held by archives in several countries. TANAP compiled an [overview of VOC-related archival collections worldwide](https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/archival-inventories/), with inventories of record groups in:

* the Nationaal Archief in The Hague (the Netherlands);
* the Arsip Nasional Republik Indonesia in Jakarta (Indonesia);
* the National Archives of Sri Lanka in Colombo (Sri Lanka);
* the Tamil Nadu Archives in Chennai (India);
* the Cape Town Archives Repository (South Africa);
* the British Library in London (United Kingdom);
* the Arkib Negara Malaysia in Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia).

The overview covers the records of the VOC's management in the Netherlands, of its central administration in Batavia and of its local administrations in Asia and Africa, as well as VOC records in private papers and other collections.

This overview is a useful reminder of how much of the VOC's record-keeping lies outside the series that Globalise works with. See also [Where are the VOC archives preserved in the present day?](/pages/research-responsibly/voc-archives-guide.md#where-are-the-voc-archives-preserved-in-the-present-day) in the guide to the VOC archives.

### TANAP index and establishment reconstructions

To make the *Overgekomen Brieven en Papieren* (OBP; in English the Copied Letters series), the series that Globalise covers, more accessible, TANAP described the individual documents in thousands of OBP volumes and assigned each document to a category. The descriptions are based on the tables of contents found in the volumes, or modelled on them where a volume had none. The document categories were created from the perspective of the VOC.

The [TANAP index and establishment reconstructions](https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/tanap-index-establishment-reconstructions/) page, written by Renate Smit (2025), explains:

* how the TANAP index was made, and where it can be consulted. The index is searchable in the [OBP index of the Nationaal Archief](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/en/research/index/nt00348), but the TANAP document categories are only available in the downloadable CSV version and in the establishment reconstructions;
* which inventory numbers the index covers. TANAP ran out of funding before it could describe all OBP volumes, and the Globalise project filled part of the gap by digitising typescript tables of contents made by the Nationaal Archief;
* the **establishment reconstructions**: lists of documents arranged by the VOC establishment that produced them and by document category, available as PDFs for 36 establishments. Because establishments did not send copies of all their documents to the Dutch Republic, these reconstructions offer a partial view of the establishments' archives.

The TANAP index is one of the sources the Globalise project used to identify and classify the documents on the portal. See [How the data was produced](/pages/research-responsibly/data/production.md#3-identifying-documents).

### Cape of Good Hope transcriptions

The TANAP website also offered transcriptions of VOC-era records kept in the Western Cape Archives and Records Service in Cape Town:

* **[Resolutions of the Council of Policy](https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/cape-transcriptions/#council-of-policy)**, the highest VOC authority at the Cape of Good Hope, 1651–1795. During the TANAP Transcription Project (2001–2003), the resolutions for 1651–1743, which had earlier been published in print with footnotes, were digitised, and those for 1744–1795 were transcribed. The page also offers an introduction by Helena Liebenberg (in English and Afrikaans), a glossary, a bibliography and a bilingual word list.
* **[Estate papers of the Orphan Chamber](https://docs.globalise.huygens.knaw.nl/tanap/cape-transcriptions/#orphan-chamber)**: inventories of deceased estates administered by the Orphan Chamber at the Cape, transcribed by the TEPC Transcription Project (2004–2008), with an introduction by the TEPC Transcription Team.

The Globalise project converted the original XML files into web pages, in which dates and the names of people, places and ships are marked. Further transcriptions concerning the VOC history of the Cape can be purchased from the [Tracing History Trust](https://tracinghistorytrust.co.za/products.htm).

These transcriptions are not part of the Globalise corpus and cannot be searched on this portal. The people recorded in the Orphan Chamber inventories are available as a structured dataset: [Overview of People in the TEPC Inventories (1673-1844)](https://doi.org/10.34894/K5BYMN).
